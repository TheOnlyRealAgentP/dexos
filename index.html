Look around

It feels like a PC that came this way.

A lot of Linux desktops make you go hunting for things. Dex OS puts what you use every day right on a home screen built for a big monitor. You never have to open a terminal unless you want to.

A home screen, on your monitor

Put your calendar, reminders, the weather and whatever's playing right on the desktop, next to the apps you open most. Add as many pages as you like. The search bar at the top finds apps and files, or just searches the web.

  • A taskbar with your favorites, recent apps and everything that's open
  • Four desktops, so work and everything else can stay apart
  • Drag a window to the edge and it snaps into place

The apps you need are already here

Right out of the box you've got a file manager, notes, a photo gallery, music, a calendar, email, a clock, the weather, a voice recorder and a screen recorder. They were all made for Dex OS, so they actually look like they belong together. That's rarer on Linux than you'd think.

Messages even lets you text from your PC through your phone.

The switches you use, one click away

Click the top-right corner and Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, dark mode, your VPN, screen recording and casting to the TV are all right there. Notifications get their own panel. Both sit on a frosted-glass blur, mostly because it looks nice.

Make it look like yours

There are 94 wallpapers built in. They're drawn instead of photographed, so they stay crisp on any monitor. Pick a color and the taskbar, menus, lock screen and apps all change to match.

Bring your apps

Switching shouldn't mean starting over.

The apps are usually what keeps people on Windows. So Dex OS runs them.

Windows programs

Got an .exe or .msi? Double-click it. Dex OS installs it with Proton, the same tech Steam uses to run Windows games, and it shows up in your apps like anything else. A few heavyweights like ArcGIS Pro and Autodesk tools need the real thing, so Dex OS can run a hidden copy of Windows just for them. Their windows still open on your desktop like normal. You'll need your own Windows license for that part.

Android apps

Same idea: double-click the .apk. Simple apps run directly. Bigger ones run inside an Android system you never have to look at. Either way, you get a normal icon and a normal window.

Dex Store

Thousands of free apps from Flathub and the Snap Store, all in one place. OBS, Blender, VS Code, Signal and plenty more are a click away.

Gaming

Install Steam? Already done.

Steam comes installed. If you've got an NVIDIA RTX or GTX 16 card, NVIDIA's driver is already on there too, and AMD and Intel graphics just work. A bunch of gaming tweaks borrowed from Nobara are turned on from the start.

Game Hub keeps your game settings in one spot. Set a frame rate cap, upscaling or a power mode once for every game, then change it for the one game that needs something different. Press Super+G mid-game to check your frame rate.

Updates

Updates shouldn't be scary.

The core of Dex OS is locked and checked every time your PC starts, so nothing can quietly mess it up. Your files, settings and programs live outside that core, and they're all yours to change however you want.

They wait for you

Updates download while you work (not on metered connections) and install the next time you restart. Either all of it goes in or none of it does.

Bad one? It goes back.

After an update, Dex OS makes sure your sign-in screen and desktop actually show up. If they don't, it switches back by itself.

Fixes don't wait around

Each part updates on its own, so a fix for Settings doesn't have to wait for a whole new version of everything.

There's always a way back

The boot menu always has a safe mode, and Settings has a button to roll back to the last version.

Privacy

Your stuff stays yours.

Encrypted drive

Your drive unlocks itself with your PC's security chip, and you get a recovery key just in case.

Fingerprint sign-in

If your laptop has a reader, go ahead and use it.

Secure Folder

Somewhere to lock away the files you really don't want anyone else seeing.

A VPN that doesn't leak

WireGuard and OpenVPN with a kill switch, so nothing slips out if the connection drops. It can even turn itself on when you join public Wi-Fi.

Auto Blocker

Someone plugs a USB stick in while your PC is locked? It stays blocked.

Passwords and passkeys

A password manager that fills things in for you in the browser comes with it.

Little things

The stuff you notice after a week.

Your cloud drives, in My Files

Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox and others show up right next to your folders. Files only download when you open them.

Your phone, on your PC

See your texts and notifications, or mirror your phone's screen.

Smart select

Draw a box around anything on screen and copy the text right out of it.

Clipboard history

Get back the thing you copied ten minutes ago, pictures and files included.

Monitors that just work

Plug one in and it runs at its best resolution and refresh rate. Super+P switches layouts.

File history

Your home folder gets a snapshot every hour, so deleting the wrong thing isn't the end of the world.

Videos just play

H.264 and HEVC work out of the box, and use your graphics card when they can.

Nice for developers, too

Install tools the normal way, no containers needed. The Claude, Gemini and Codex CLIs keep their keys locked in the security chip.

Accessible from the start

The screen reader is ready before you've set up anything at all.

Try it

Take it for a spin first.

  1. 1
    Put it on a USB stick. Fedora Media Writer, balenaEtcher or Rufus (in DD mode) all work. Grab a stick that's 16 GB or bigger.
  2. 2
    Start your PC from the stick and pick "Try without installing." Nothing on your PC changes, so poke around as long as you like.
  3. 3
    Like it? Hit Install. It takes about 10 minutes. Replace Windows completely, or keep it around next to Dex OS if you're not ready to let go.

What you'll need

  • A 64-bit PC with UEFI, which is pretty much anything from the last ten years
  • A USB stick, 16 GB or bigger
  • NVIDIA, AMD or Intel graphics. Older GeForce cards (GTX 900 and 10 series) work, but games run slower on them.
  • Secure Boot turned off, for now. Signed builds are planned.
